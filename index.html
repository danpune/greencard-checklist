1First week

Quick things to do as soon as the card arrives.

Check your name, date of birth, country of birth, and the "Resident Since" date. That date starts your citizenship clock. If anything is wrong, fix it with Form I-90 (no fee if the error was made by DHS/USCIS).

uscis.gov/i-90

Scan the card (front and back) and the I-797 approval notice. Keep your old visas, I-94s and work permit (EAD) in the same folder.

The law requires this for everyone 18 and older, and not carrying it can be a misdemeanor. It's enforced more strictly now. Keep the original on you and a copy at home.

uscis.gov/alienregistration uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/rights-and-responsibilities-of-a-green-card-holder-permanent-resident

Use it to track cases and update your address.

myaccount.uscis.gov

Most cards are good for 10 years. Renew with Form I-90 in the 6 months before it expires. Your status doesn't end when the card expires, but you need a valid card to work, travel and prove status. A 2-year card (usually marriage-based) is different: file Form I-751 in the 90 days before it expires.

uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card uscis.gov/i-751

2Work

Let your employer know, and enjoy the new freedom.

Tell HR and the immigration team you've moved from H-1B/L-1/EAD to a green card, so they stop tracking visa expiry dates. Ask the attorney to close any pending H-1B filings. Once your green card is on file, your employer shouldn't ask you to re-verify it when it expires.

uscis.gov/i-9-central/form-i-9-resources/handbook-for-employers-m-274/70-evidence-of-employment-authorization-for-certain-categories/71-lawful-permanent-residents-lpr
💡

Thinking of changing jobs? If your green card came through an employer, it's common to stay about 6 months after approval to show you meant to take the job. That's a good habit, not a law. You're now free to change jobs, freelance, do side gigs, or start an LLC (talk to a CPA first), and there's no more visa stamping.

3IDs & eligibility

Update your IDs, and avoid the one mistake that can't be undone.

This removes the "Valid for work only with DHS authorization" line. Your old card plus your green card is still fine for work. Start online and SSA will book an appointment. Bring your green card and passport. It's free.

ssa.gov/personal-record/update-citizenship-or-immigration-status

Your old license may expire with your visa. Take your green card to the DMV and ask for the longest validity your state allows. Make sure it's a REAL ID. For domestic flights you need a REAL ID or another accepted ID, like your green card or passport.

usa.gov/state-motor-vehicle-services tsa.gov/realid

Men must register before their 26th birthday (ages 18 through 25). Skipping it can cause problems on your citizenship application.

sss.gov/register

This is Form AR-11. Your USCIS online account is the fastest way to do it.

uscis.gov/addresschange
⚠️

Never register to vote or vote. DMV and other forms sometimes ask about voter registration. Say no. Never tick "U.S. citizen" on any form either (I-9, loans, job applications). This is one of the few mistakes that can cost you the green card and citizenship for good. usa.gov/who-can-vote

🌿

Marijuana is still illegal under federal law, even in states where it's legal. Using it, or working in the cannabis industry, can hurt your citizenship application and your green card. uscis.gov/archive/uscis-issues-policy-guidance-clarifying-how-federal-controlled-substances-law-applies-to

4Travel

Always carry your passport and green card. No U.S. visa needed to come back.

Under 6 monthsFine.
6 to 12 monthsUSCIS assumes your "continuous residence" for citizenship is broken unless you can prove strong U.S. ties. Expect questions at the airport.
1 year or moreBreaks continuous residence. Without a re-entry permit, your green card is generally treated as given up.

Write down every date you leave and come back. You'll need it for citizenship. Check it against CBP's "Travel History" records.

i94.cbp.dhs.gov/search/history-search

Green card holders can apply. It's $120 for 5 years, and many travel credit cards pay it back. Tip from Reddit: once you're conditionally approved, do the interview through "Enrollment on Arrival" when you land from an international trip. It's much faster than waiting for an appointment.

ttp.cbp.dhs.gov cbp.gov/travel/trusted-traveler-programs/global-entry/eligibility cbp.gov/travel/trusted-traveler-programs/global-entry/enrollment-arrival

If you might be away close to a year or longer, apply for a re-entry permit (Form I-131) while you're still in the U.S. Plan to stay for the biometrics appointment. It's usually good for up to 2 years. It protects your green card, but not your citizenship clock.

uscis.gov/i-131 uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/international-travel-as-a-permanent-resident
🚫

Never sign Form I-407 at the airport without talking to a lawyer. Signing it gives up your green card. It's voluntary, and you have the right to see an immigration judge instead. uscis.gov/i-407

🌍

From a country on the 2025–26 travel restriction list? Green card holders are exempt from the entry ban. But USCIS has added extra screening for people from those countries, so expect more checks on filings like I-90, I-751 and N-400. uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/update-on-uscis-strengthened-screening-and-vetting

🇨🇦

Going to Canada? Green card holders don't need an eTA. Just carry your passport and green card. canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/eta/eligibility.html

5Money & taxes

What you file now is looked at when you apply for citizenship.

Report worldwide income. Never file as a nonresident, and don't claim to be a resident on a tax return in another country. Either can be treated as giving up your green card. Keep every return; the citizenship application asks about them.

irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/introduction-to-residency-under-us-tax-law

After you file each year, download that year's transcript into your citizenship folder. It's the IRS's own record of what you filed: not the same as filing, and not your copy of the return. Free.

  • N-400: bring 5 years (3 on the marriage path). USCIS's checklist requires them after any trip of 6 months or more; on the marriage path they're one accepted way to document the marriage. Commonly requested at the interview.
  • Why yearly: the "tax return transcript" only covers the current and 3 prior years.
  • Behind? The "tax account transcript" in your IRS online account covers the current and 9 prior years. By mail: current and 3 prior, in 5 to 10 days.
irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript irs.gov: transcript types uscis.gov: N-400 checklist (M-477)

If your foreign accounts add up to more than $10K at any point in the year, file an FBAR. Larger balances also need Form 8938 with your tax return: $50K at year-end or $75K at any time if single, $100K or $150K if married filing jointly.

fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements

More lenders and loan programs are open to you now, so it's worth getting fresh quotes. Your rate still depends on credit, income and down payment.

6Road to citizenship (N-400)

Eligible after 5 years, or 3 if married to and living with a U.S. citizen. You can file 90 days early.

This counts back 90 days from your 5-year (or 3-year) anniversary, as USCIS allows. Double-check with the official tool before filing: uscis.gov/citizenship-resource-center/learn-about-citizenship/naturalization-eligibility-tool-0

Trip tracker

Add each trip outside the U.S. It flags long trips and adds up your days abroad. Saved on this device only.

    • Continuous residence (a trip of 6 months or more can break it, see Travel)
    • Physically in the U.S. at least 30 of the 60 months (18 of 36 on the 3-year path)
    • Lived in your state for 3 months before filing
    • Good moral character
    • English test + civics test
    uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-d-chapter-3 uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-d-chapter-4 uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-d-chapter-6
    • Travel log
    • 5 years of tax returns, plus IRS tax transcripts (see Money & taxes)
    • Home addresses and employers for the last 5 years
    • Papers for any tickets or court issues

    For applications filed on or after Oct 20, 2025: 128 questions to study, 20 asked, 12 correct to pass.

    uscis.gov/citizenship-resource-center/naturalization-test-and-study-resources/2025-civics-test

    The fee is $710 online or $760 on paper. Check the current fee before you file.

    uscis.gov/n-400 uscis.gov/g-1055?form=n-400
    ⚠️

    Stricter since 2025. USCIS looks harder at "good moral character": taxes, tickets, and honesty on past forms. Neighborhood and employer checks are back in some cases, and social media may be reviewed. File taxes on time, pay tickets, and keep your answers the same on every form. uscis.gov/policy-manual/updates

    7What your green card unlocks

    Good to know, nothing to tick off.

    • Sponsor family: you can petition for your spouse and unmarried children (Form I-130). usa.gov/sponsor-family-member
    • Work freely: any employer, freelancing, or your own business.
    • Student aid: you can get federal student aid. In-state tuition depends on your state's rules. studentaid.gov/understand-aid/eligibility/requirements/non-us-citizens
    • Health coverage: you can buy Marketplace plans. Many public benefits have a 5-year wait. healthcare.gov/immigrants/lawfully-present-immigrants
