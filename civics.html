        Reading and writing practice

        In the interview you read 1 sentence out of 3 aloud, and write 1 out of 3 as the officer says it. USCIS does not publish the test sentences, so these are practice sentences that use only words from its official vocabulary lists.

        What the interview includes

        One appointment with a USCIS officer. It has three parts, and the civics test is only one of them.

        1. Your application, under oath

        The officer puts you under oath and goes through the questions and answers on your N-400. Re-read your own application before you go, and note anything that changed since you filed, such as travel, address, job or marital status.

        2. The English test

        SpeakingJudged during the conversation about your application. You pass if you generally understand and respond meaningfully. You may ask for a question to be repeated or rephrased.
        ReadingRead 1 sentence out of 3 correctly, out loud.
        WritingWrite 1 sentence out of 3 as the officer dictates it. Spelling, capital letters and punctuation mistakes don't fail you unless they change the meaning. Don't abbreviate words.

        3. The civics test

        Filed N-400 on or after Oct 20, 20252025 test, the one on this page. 128 questions to study. Asked up to 20, out loud. The officer stops at 12 right (pass) or 9 wrong (fail).
        Filed before Oct 20, 20252008 test. 100 questions to study, asked up to 10, 6 right to pass. Use the official 2008 practice test instead of this page.

        A different wording of a correct answer still counts.

        If you don't pass a part

        You get one retest, 60 to 90 days after the first interview, on only the part you failed. Missing the retest counts as a failed attempt. Failing a second time means the application is denied.

        After the interview

        You leave with a results notice (Form N-652). The decision is granted, continued or denied, and USCIS has 120 days from the interview to decide. You are not a citizen until you take the Oath of Allegiance at a ceremony, which may be the same day or scheduled later by mail. You hand in your green card at the ceremony.

        Who is exempt

        50/20Age 50 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident when you file. No English test. Civics test in your own language, with an interpreter you bring.
        55/15Age 55 or older with 15 years as a permanent resident. Same as above.
        65/20Age 65 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident. No English test. Study only the 20 starred questions: asked 10, need 6.
        MedicalA physical or developmental disability or mental impairment lasting 12 months or more may exempt you from English, civics or both. A licensed doctor or clinical psychologist completes Form N-648, which goes in with the N-400.

        How to prepare

        About 20 minutes a day for four weeks is a reasonable plan for a working adult.

        A four-week plan

        Week 1Questions 1 to 45, about 9 new ones a day. Enter your ZIP code above and learn your four personal answers. Read the reading vocabulary list aloud once.
        Week 2Questions 46 to 90, plus 5 minutes a day on what you missed. Write 5 dictated sentences a day from the writing vocabulary list. One mock test at the weekend.
        Week 3Questions 91 to 128, then your missed list only. Do the three USCIS interview vocabulary exercises. Two mock tests.
        Week 4Missed list and a mock test every other day, answered out loud. Re-read your own N-400. Check the four officials' names a few days before the interview.

        On the 65/20 list? Two weeks of the same routine on the 20 starred questions is enough.

        Habits that help

        • Say your answers out loud. The real test is a conversation, not a form.
        • Learn one short answer per question. You only need one unless the question asks for more.
        • Study a little every day and let the questions you know drop out.
        • Know your own N-400 as well as the civics answers.
