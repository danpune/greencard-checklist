        Practice the interview

        The officer puts you under oath (you promise to tell the truth) and asks the questions from your Form N-400 out loud. Practice understanding them here.

        This page helps you understand the questions. It cannot tell you how to answer them, and it is not legal advice. If you are not sure how a question applies to you, talk to an immigration attorney before your interview.

        At the interview

        One appointment with a USCIS officer. Tap a heading to open it.

        What happens at the interview

        1. Your application, under oath

        The officer puts you under oath and goes through the questions and answers on your N-400. Re-read your own application before you go, and note anything that changed since you filed, such as travel, address, job or marital status.

        2. The English test

        SpeakingJudged during the conversation about your application. You pass if you generally understand and respond meaningfully. You may ask for a question to be repeated or rephrased.
        ReadingRead 1 sentence out of 3 correctly, out loud.
        WritingWrite 1 sentence out of 3 as the officer says it. Spelling, capital letters and punctuation mistakes don't fail you unless they change the meaning. Don't abbreviate words.

        3. The civics test

        Filed N-400 on or after Oct 20, 20252025 test, the one on this page. 128 questions to study. Asked up to 20, out loud. The officer stops at 12 right (pass) or 9 wrong (fail).
        Filed before Oct 20, 20252008 test. 100 questions to study, asked up to 10, 6 right to pass. Use the official 2008 practice test instead of this page.

        A different wording of a correct answer still counts.

        If you don't pass, and what comes after

        If you don't pass a part

        You get one retest, 60 to 90 days after the first interview, on only the part you failed. Missing the retest counts as a failed attempt, unless USCIS excuses it. Failing a second time means the application is denied.

        After the interview

        You leave with a results notice (Form N-652). The decision is granted, continued or denied, and USCIS has 120 days from the interview to decide. You are not a citizen until you take the Oath of Allegiance at a ceremony. The ceremony may be on the same day as your interview. If not, USCIS mails you a notice. You hand in your green card at the ceremony.

        Who is exempt from the tests
        50/20Age 50 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident when you file. No English test. Civics test in your own language, with an interpreter you bring.
        55/15Age 55 or older with 15 years as a permanent resident. Same as above.
        65/20Age 65 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident. No English test. Study only the 20 starred questions: asked 10, need 6. You may take it in your own language, with an interpreter you bring.
        MedicalA physical or developmental disability or a mental impairment may exempt you from English, civics or both. It must have lasted, or be expected to last, 12 months or more. A licensed doctor or clinical psychologist completes Form N-648, which you send together with Form N-400.

        How to prepare

        About 20 minutes a day for four weeks is a reasonable plan for a working adult.

        A four-week study plan
        Week 1Questions 1 to 45, about 9 new ones a day. Enter your ZIP code above and learn your four personal answers. Read the reading vocabulary list aloud once.
        Week 2Questions 46 to 90, plus 5 minutes a day on what you missed. Listen to and write 5 sentences a day from the writing vocabulary list. One mock test on the weekend.
        Week 3Questions 91 to 128, then your missed list only. Do the two USCIS interview vocabulary self-tests and the commands exercise. Two mock tests.
        Week 4Missed list and a mock test every other day, answered out loud. Re-read your own N-400. Check the four officials' names a few days before the interview.

        On the 65/20 list? Two weeks of the same routine on the 20 starred questions is a reasonable plan.

        Habits that help

        • Say your answers out loud. The real test is a conversation, not a form.
        • Learn one short answer per question. You only need one unless the question asks for more.
        • Study a little every day, and spend less time on the questions you already know.
        • Know your own N-400 as well as the civics answers.
